MyDSM Rules

Who can use MyDSM

  • You must be 16 years old or older to use MyDSM.
  • If it is shown that an account belongs to someone under 16, that account is banned permanently. There is no second chance on this rule.
  • One person, real accounts. No fake accounts, and no new accounts to get around a suspension or a ban.

What you can share

  • MyDSM is a 16+ place. Swearing, mature topics, dark humour and flirty jokes are fine.
  • No explicit nudity and no sexual content: no naked pictures, no porn, no sexual pictures or descriptions, in chats, files, display pictures or profiles.
  • Anything sexual involving someone under 18 is never allowed. The account is banned permanently and the content can be reported to the police.
  • No gore, no shocking violence, no content that encourages self-harm.
  • Nothing illegal: no selling drugs or weapons, no pirated files, no scams.

How you treat people

  • Be kind. No harassment, bullying, threats or hate against anyone, including for their race, religion, gender, sexuality, disability or origin.
  • Do not share other people's private information (address, phone, photos, real name) without their permission.
  • Do not pretend to be someone else, and never pretend to be MyDSM staff or @nanachi.
  • No spam, chain messages, fake links, viruses or attempts to steal accounts.

Your profile and pictures

  • Your display name, personal message, display picture and profile page follow the same rules as your messages.
  • Staff can ask you to change something on your profile. If you ignore it, they can change or remove it for you, or take further action.

Moderation

  • Anyone can report a chat, a group conversation or a profile. Reports are read by the MyDSM moderators.
  • Moderators only see what was reported: the last messages of the reported conversation, or the reported profile. They cannot read your other conversations.
  • In serious cases (for example the safety of a minor), an administrator can read a conversation. They must write down why, and every access is recorded.
  • Depending on how serious it is, staff can send you a warning, mute you, suspend your account for a while, or ban it permanently.
  • If you think a decision was wrong, you can ask an administrator to look at it again.

Your account

  • You are responsible for what happens on your account. Keep your password and your recovery key to yourself.
  • Your recovery key is the way to reset a forgotten password. Keep it somewhere safe. If you lose both, an administrator can help, but only after you prove the account is yours.
MyDSM Privacy Policy

What MyDSM keeps

  • Your @username, display name, personal message, display picture and profile page.
  • Your password, stored only as a secure hash. Nobody can read it, not even staff.
  • Your e-mail address only if you gave one. It is never shown to anyone.
  • Your messages, group conversations and shared files, encrypted on the way and encrypted when stored on the server.
  • Which rules version you accepted and when.
  • A log of what staff do (warnings, bans, conversations read and why).

Who can see what

  • Your contacts see your status, personal message, picture and your "What's new" posts.
  • Your profile page is visible to your contacts, or to every member if you choose so.
  • Messages are not end-to-end encrypted. This is on purpose, so that reports can be checked and people kept safe. Staff access is limited as described in the Rules, and is always logged.
  • People find you by your @username. You can hide yourself from people search in Options.

How long it is kept

  • Files sent in chats are deleted after 7 days.
  • Messages stay until you or your contact delete your account.
  • When you delete your account, your profile, contacts, messages and files are deleted. Nightly server backups are kept for 14 days and then removed.
  • Server logs with IP addresses are kept for a short time (about two weeks) to protect MyDSM against attacks.

What MyDSM never does

  • No ads, no trackers, and your data is never sold or shared with anyone.
  • The only exception: when the law requires it, or to protect a child, information can be given to the police.

Questions

  • MyDSM is a free, non-profit project run by @nanachi. Ask any question to an administrator inside MyDSM.
